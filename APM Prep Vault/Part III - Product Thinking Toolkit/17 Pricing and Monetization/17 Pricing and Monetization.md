# 17 Pricing and Monetization

Pricing questions show up in APM interviews as "how would you price X", "which monetization model fits Y", and "how would you test a price change". Strong answers start from the business goal and the customer's willingness to pay, then pick a model, then describe how to validate it.

This chapter covers three things: how to choose a price basis ([[17.1 Cost-plus, value-based, and competitive pricing]]), how to pick a monetization model ([[17.2 Monetization models - subscription, freemium, ads, take rate, razor-and-blade, bundling]]), and how to test pricing safely ([[17.3 Running pricing experiments]]).

## Subsection notes
- [[17.1 Cost-plus, value-based, and competitive pricing]]: three ways to anchor a price, and when each one applies.
- [[17.2 Monetization models - subscription, freemium, ads, take rate, razor-and-blade, bundling]]: the main revenue models and their trade-offs.
- [[17.3 Running pricing experiments]]: how to test a price with real users without damaging trust.

## Key takeaways
- Start from the business goal (market share or profitability) before picking a price, and triangulate cost (floor), competitors (reference) and value (ceiling).
- Value-based pricing is usually the strongest anchor, but it needs evidence of willingness to pay.
- Freemium works only when the free tier brings users in and the paid tier serves a clear need.
- Pick the monetization model that matches where value is created: seats, usage, transactions or outcomes.
- Test prices with a holdout or a limited cohort, and watch retention and churn as well as conversion.
