# 14 Prioritization

Prioritisation is where a product manager turns many ideas into a decision. Frameworks make the reasoning visible, so stakeholders can agree on what to build next and why. This chapter covers five common scoring and sorting tools and how to choose and defend one.

## Subsections
- [[14.1 RICE]] - scores reach, impact, confidence and effort into one comparable number.
- [[14.2 Value vs effort]] - a 2x2 matrix that sorts ideas into quick wins, big bets, fill-ins and money pits.
- [[14.3 Kano model]] - classifies features as expected (must-be), performance, exciting (attractive) or indifferent.
- [[14.4 MoSCoW]] - sorts scope into Must, Should, Could and Won't for a release.
- [[14.5 ICE]] - a fast Impact, Confidence and Ease score, suited to growth experiments.
- [[14.6 Choosing and defending a framework]] - how to match a framework to the decision and explain the trade-off.

## Key takeaways
- A framework makes trade-offs visible. It does not replace judgement.
- RICE = Reach x Impact x Confidence / Effort (Intercom); it is the most precise for a large backlog. ICE is the fastest.
- Kano explains satisfaction; MoSCoW defines scope; value vs effort gives a quick visual.
- Always state the weakest assumption behind a score, especially confidence.
- In an interview, name the decision, pick a framework, show the inputs, and say how you would revisit it.
