# 18 Go-to-Market and Launch

Launch questions in APM interviews ask you to plan how a product reaches users: who the first users are, how you roll it out, which channels you use, and how you know it worked. A good answer covers the timeline (before, during, after launch) and the decisions at each step.

This chapter covers the launch timeline ([[18.1 Pre-launch, launch, and post-launch activities]]), how to de-risk a launch with pilots and phased rollouts ([[18.2 Pilots, MVPs, and phased rollouts]]), how to choose distribution ([[18.3 Partnerships and distribution channels]]), and how to define success before you launch ([[18.4 Launch success criteria]]).

## Subsection notes
- [[18.1 Pre-launch, launch, and post-launch activities]]: what to do before, during and after a launch.
- [[18.2 Pilots, MVPs, and phased rollouts]]: how to test with a small audience and widen the rollout.
- [[18.3 Partnerships and distribution channels]]: how partners and channels change reach and cost.
- [[18.4 Launch success criteria]]: how to set measurable success and failure thresholds.

## Key takeaways
- A GTM strategy defines the ICP, competitors, messaging, targets, tactics and feedback loops.
- Choose product-led or sales-led GTM based on how the buyer discovers and pays for the product.
- Launch in phases: an MVP or pilot first, then a wider rollout gated on metrics.
- Set success criteria before launch, with a clear threshold for rollback.
- Partnerships and channels can accelerate distribution, but they cost margin or control.
