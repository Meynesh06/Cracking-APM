# 25.3 Recording and reviewing your own answers
> You cannot see your own structure while you speak. A recording plus a fixed review routine shows the patterns that peers and timers miss.

## Concept
**Record every drill.** Use a phone voice memo (or video, if you want to check eye contact and pacing). Save the file with the date, case name and score from the rubric in [[25.2 Peer mock interviews and a feedback rubric]].

**Review within 24 hours.** Listen at 1.25x speed. Do not stop to fix every word. Note time stamps where you:
- Lost the thread or repeated yourself.
- Stated a number without a basis.
- Skipped metrics or guardrails.
- Ran past a time checkpoint from [[25.1 Timed solo drill (20 minutes)]].

**Error log.** Keep one table with these columns: date, case, error type (structure, clarity, depth, metrics, time, AI judgement), what happened, fix. Patterns show up quickly; after five entries, the most common error type is your focus for the next week.

**Re-attempt.** Redo the same case one week later, using only the error log as a checklist. Compare the rubric scores. An improvement on the re-attempt matters more than a good first attempt on a new case.

**Weekly cadence.**

| Day | Activity |
|---|---|
| Mon | Timed drill on a new case from [[24 AI Case Bank]] |
| Tue | Review recording, update error log |
| Thu | Peer mock with rubric scoring ([[25.2 Peer mock interviews and a feedback rubric]]) |
| Sat | Re-attempt last Monday's case using only the error log, then compare rubric scores |

**Use the vault.** Read the model answer only after your own attempt, then compare what you missed. Keep notes short. The point is to change your next answer, not to build a bigger document.

## Real-world examples
- **Compare against a model answer:** after a recorded attempt at a casebook case, check your recording against the condensed solution in [[23.1 Sigma casebook solutions]]. For example, did you define "active driver" before diagnosing Uber Drivers, and did you find the time-of-day cut?
- **Check metric precision:** the Google Photos report in [[23.3 Annotated answers from interview experiences]] says interviewers want "active users bounced per hour", not "bounce rate". Every vague metric in your recording is an error-log entry.

## Practice questions
1. Why review within 24 hours rather than at the end of the week?
   - *Outline:* memory of what you were thinking fades quickly, and the recording still has the reasoning fresh.
2. What makes an error log more useful than a list of mistakes?
   - *Outline:* the fix column turns each error into a rule you apply in the next drill.
