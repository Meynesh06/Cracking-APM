# 6.5 Copilot vs automation vs autonomous agent
> Autonomy is a dial: the more the AI acts without a human, the more value it can create and the more risk, cost and evaluation burden you take on.

## Concept
Three product postures sit on one spectrum:

| Posture | Who decides | Example behaviour | Typical risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copilot (assistive) | Human, AI suggests | Drafts text, code completion, suggested replies | Low; the human catches errors |
| Automation (rules + AI) | AI executes a bounded step | Auto-tags tickets, routes a claim | Medium; errors repeat at scale |
| Autonomous agent | AI plans and executes multi-step tasks | Books, files, or resolves end to end using tools | High; wrong actions have real consequences |

Why it matters for strategy and economics:
- **Value:** autonomy removes more human effort, so it can justify higher prices (outcome-based pricing fits here).
- **Cost:** agents call models many times per task (plan, act, check), so cost per task rises; see 6.2.
- **Evaluation:** a copilot is judged on acceptance; an agent needs end-to-end task success, step-level traces and safety evals (see 8.1 and 8.4).
- **Trust:** moving down the spectrum requires earned confidence, typically by starting as a copilot, logging accept/edit rates, then widening scope.
- **Reversibility:** actions that are reversible (drafts) can be autonomous sooner than irreversible ones (payments, emails sent to customers).

Analogy that works in interviews: self-driving cars' levels of autonomy (driver assistance to full self-driving). Waymo runs fully driverless robotaxis in limited areas after years of testing and remote support; most cars ship only driver assistance. AI products climb the same ladder, area by area.

A PM decision rule: start at the lowest autonomy that delivers the value, earn the right to go higher with measured acceptance and error data, and put approval gates on any irreversible step.

## Real-world examples
- **GitHub Copilot** - the canonical copilot: suggestions appear in the editor and the developer accepts or rejects them.
- **Intercom Fin** - a support agent that resolves conversations end to end for many queries, with handoff to humans for the rest (agent-like, scoped by intent).
- **Cognition's Devin** - announced in March 2024 as an autonomous software engineering agent; independent tests reported low end-to-end success on real tasks, which shows how hard the high-autonomy end is to evaluate.
- **Ticket auto-triage in help desks (Zendesk, Freshdesk)** - AI classifies and routes tickets: bounded automation, where an error repeats at scale but is easy to reverse.
- **Duolingo Max** - "Explain my answer" is a copilot-style feature that keeps the learner in control.

## Practice questions
1. You are asked to add an AI agent that can issue refunds in a food delivery app. Copilot, automation, or agent? What guardrails?
   - Outline: start with a copilot that drafts the refund for agent approval; automate small refunds under a threshold with rollback and audit; require approval above a limit.
2. How does moving from copilot to agent change your metrics?
   - Outline: from acceptance and edit rate to task success, escalation rate, and cost per completed task.
3. Why can a higher-autonomy product have worse unit economics?
   - Outline: more model calls per task, retries and verification steps, monitoring and human review of failures, and the cost of errors (refunds, support), all of which can outweigh the higher price.
4. What would make you say no to an autonomous feature?
   - Outline: irreversible or high-harm actions, no reliable way to evaluate end-to-end success, regulation requiring human decision-making (credit, hiring), or error costs that exceed the labour saved.
