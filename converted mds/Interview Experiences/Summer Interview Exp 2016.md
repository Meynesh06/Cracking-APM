Shreyas Joshi, Product Manager

1.  Describe  the  steps  in  the  process  i.e.  no  of  interview  rounds,  GD,  pre-process

requirements (LOI, case submission etc.)

a.  Pre-process  requirement  is  to  write  a  one-pager  Statement  of  Purpose  (SOP)  outlining
which  role  you’re  applying  to,  and  why  you’re  a  proper  fit  for  the  role  –  there’s  no
predefined  format  but  they  check  for  a  role-applicant  fit  and  evaluate  the  SOP  based  on
how closely it matches with Amazon’s Leadership Principles

b.  There’s no GD
c.  The number of interview rounds vary from person to person on a case-by-case basis. In my
case,  I  only  had  one  personal  interview  while  others  had  3-4  interviews.  The  average
interview rounds are 2, where the first round is they decide which role you’re a best fit for, and
the next rounds are elimination rounds

2.  Does work experience matter?

a.  Not in the strictest sense, but Amazon has a lot of roles to be offered (this year they came

to campus with 9 roles)

b.  Each role has a preferred work experience, but they don’t adhere to it strictly and are quite

flexible with the exact duration of work experience.

c.  The interview is based on your work experience majorly if you have worked for 2+ years in

the industry (not necessarily IT or Technology sector)

3.  What  kind  of  questions  were  asked?  What  is  the  general  area  of  focus  during  the

interviews?
PM Topics Covered: Behavioral

a.  The questions asked to me were more in the sense of “life-stories” wherein they check for

the authenticity of the points written in SOP.

b.  Expect deep probing into any question that you’re asked – how you displayed ownership, how
you resolved conflicts and they do not want globe, in the sense that you should talk about
something only if you have a certain level of involvement and expertise in the topic.

c.  The general area of focus is again trying to understand the kind of roles that interest you
and they’ll also suggest alternate teams that they think are a good fit for you, based on their
preliminary  analysis – it’s  important to know  what  you want and  more importantly, what
you DO NOT want.
In  my  case,  I  was  offered  to  be  part  of  the  MLDP  role  (Marketplace  Leadership
Development  Programme),  but  since  I  was  focused  on  Product  Management,  I  explicitly
told  them  the  same.  They  do  take  into  account  personal  preferences,  but  you  need  to
justify your choices.

d.

4.  What kind of HR questions were asked?
a.  No HR questions were asked to me.
b.  For other candidates, HR questions mostly revolved around their SOP – the strengths and

weaknesses that they listed.

5.  How do you recommend a PGP1 should structure his preparation? Resources/ tools that

can help aid in preparation?

a.  The preparation should involve being up-to-date with latest happenings in the tech industry

– particularly the e-commerce sector.

b.  Subscribe to newsletters provided by TechCrunch, Wired, Inc42, NextBigWhat and the like
where you will know the latest technologies and their applications in various firms around
the world.
Important is to have interest in the technology field, although you may not have a
Computer Science degree. They’ll check for authenticity and frankness in the interviews.

c.

6.  Any Feedback/comments for PGP1s:

a.  Try to talk to PGP2s who have interned there, and match your expectations and demands

before applying.

b.  The culture at Amazon is different as well, so if you’re the sort of person who connects with

that, and make your decision accordingly.

c.  Summers are one of the best ways to get into the company, and PPO rates vary but highly

dependent on your personal potential.

Tulasi Vijaya Kumar, Product Manager

1.  Describe  the  steps  in  the  process  i.e.  no  of  interview  rounds,  GD,  pre-process

requirements (LOI, case submission etc.)
Pre-Process  Requirements:  Once  Amazon visits  the  campus  and  opens  the  roles  they  are  going  to
offer at the summers, every candidate will be given an opportunity to opt for two roles out of the
roles opened at the campus. Candidates must send SOPs for the roles they are applying for.
GD: No GD.
Rounds of Interviews: Depends on the first interview, the subsequent rounds will be decided. I had
just  one  round.  Amazon  even  took  four  rounds  for  some  candidates.  These  interviews  can  be  a
blend of technical and HR.

2.  Does work experience matter?

Yes, work experience matters at least for some of the roles offered like Product Management and
Program  Management.  Apart  from  a  couple  of  roles  that  were  offered  almost  every  role’s  job
description mentioned work experience as desirable or made as part of eligibility criteria.

3.  What  kind  of  questions  were  asked?  What  is  the  general  area  of  focus  during  the

interviews?
PM Topics Covered: Product Strategy, Guesstimates
Case related to Amazon Prime was given to me to analyze the target segment, how to implement
the  Amazon  Prime  strategy  in  India  in  what  geographies  and  what  product  categories  are  more
appropriate  to  be  covered  by  Amazon  Prime.  Interviewer  summed  up  the  discussion  by  asking  a
guesstimate  on  pricing  of  Amazon  Prime.  So  it  was  more  like  a  case  interview  where  the  primary
emphasis was on structure, thought process, communication and conviction. Analytical thinking and
problem  solving  is  what  Amazon  looks  for  in  candidates.  You  will  be  given  a  sheet  of  paper  and
interviewer will be looking at the end how systematically and structurally candidate approaches the
problem.

4.  What kind of HR questions were asked?

Once the case discussion came to an end there were questions on behavioral and situational based
questions  which  are  mostly  related  to  HR.  For  candidates  with  work  experience  it  is  advisable  to
keep the incidents and experiences related to previous work experience ready as candidate will be
tested  with  questions  like  ‘incidents  when  you  outperformed  or  solved  a  critical  problem  in  the
previous job’. Apart from this I was asked generic expected question such as strengths, personal life and
professional background.

5.  How do you recommend a PGP1 should structure his preparation? Resources/ tools that

can help aid in preparation?
Case preparation will come handy in case of Amazon interview. Candidate should be aware of the
recent technical developments. One thing that impresses the interviewer for sure is linking answers
to the Amazon’s 12 Management Principles and ensuring they are embedded in answers.

6.  Any Feedback/comments for PGP1s:

Know-how about Amazon, their recent acquisitions and new services they are coming up with like
Amazon Pantry, Amazon Pay etc. Cases that are given by Amazon would be mostly the ones related
to their product, about their website or the recent technical/business problem solved by them. So
decent  understanding  about  Amazon’s  products/services,  business  and  Amazon’s  12  Management
Principles is what makes candidate interview-ready.

7.  How was the internship experience, work culture and PPO policy?

PPO Policy: Yes (Yet to be released for last year summers)
Internship  experience  and  work  culture:  I  did  my  internship  in  one  of  the  most  rampant  growing
areas  of  Amazon  and  was  supported  well  by  the  Manager  and  the  team.  No  long  working  hours
(Flexible work-hours and work from home is possible) but candidate is expected to be accountable
for  the  assignment  being  given.  Most  of  the  candidates  were  given  more  than  one  project  so
planning from Day 1 is quintessential for completing the project on time and to avoid running from
pillar  to  post  in  the  pursuit  of  meeting  stakeholders  of  the  project.  Candidate  has  to  be  good  at
written communication as everything including new business plans/product or feature launches will
be presented only in the form of a 6-page narrative and PPTs are not in use. So the successful PPO
conversion depends a lot on writing concise, effective and data-driven narrative which is the desired
form for final project report of summer internship.

Vandana, Product Manager

1.  Describe  the  steps  in  the  process  i.e.  no  of  interview  rounds,  GD,  pre-process

requirements (LOI, case submission etc.)
Amazon’s  process  is  very  simple.  Just  had  to  apply  on  the  PlaceCom  portal.  The  shortlist  was
released  on  the  GD  day  at  2am  at  night.  The  placement  process  started  at  8am.  The  process  was
twofold. First, there was 20-30min HR round. Then there was a technical round.

2.  Does work experience matter?

It does matter. They did not say that explicitly. But, I felt that they prefer people with experience.

3.  What  kind  of  questions  were  asked?  What  is  the  general  area  of  focus  during  the

interviews?
PM Topics Covered: Product Strategy
In the technical round, they told me about a recommendation kind of plan and asked how would I
go about it. It mostly involved marketing concepts. This may vary with the products they ask about.
Not  much  of  technical  knowledge  is  required  if  you  have  a  basic  understanding  of  marketing
concepts. I think they expect very straightforward and honest answers.

4.  What kind of HR questions were asked?

They  asked  questions  like  “Tell  me  more  about  yourself”,  “Tell  about  a  kind  of  situation  that  you
faced”,  “Why  do  you  want  to  join  the  company?”,  “If  you  have  to  come  up  with  a  product  what
would  it  be?” etc. They  basically want to know whether  you  have  understood the  job description
correctly and whether you fit in the company. They may pick out a few things from your CV and ask
about what were the learnings, challenges etc. from that etc.

5.  How do you recommend a PGP1 should structure his preparation? Resources/ tools that

can help aid in preparation?
Knowing about the products of the company is very important. If you have some contacts inside the
company then ask them about recent happenings there. Understanding of the product management
role is important. There are many other roles like category manager, program manager etc. which
might create confusion and you might deviate from the role while being in the interview itself. You
should know the scope and boundaries of product management.

6.  Any Feedback/comments for PGP1s:

Not with  respect  to only  Amazon,  I think guesstimate is  one topic  which  everyone  should prepare
not only for consults because it can be used for your analysis. I used it even in my Microsoft GD.

7.  How was the internship experience, work culture and PPO policy?

The objective and goals in Amazon are very customer-centric. Your suggestions are expected to be
simple and you should always keep the customers in your mind. The way of writing is very different.
You should create 6-page documents which are unique in structure instead of presentations. If you
get selected make sure that you learn that very quickly. People are very busy with their work there
and you should be proactive and ask for time if you need some information. The PPO list is not out
yet.

5.  What kind of questions were asked? What is the general focus of the interview?

PM Topics Covered: Behavioral
Amazon  has  no  GD  or  written  test.  Every  shortlisted  candidate  gets  a  chance  at  interview.
Multiple profiles are offered and only 2 profiles can be applied for.

Amazon  is  excessively  focused  on  its  leadership  principles  (google  them)  and  is  looking  for
candidates that are a cultural fit in that framework. Most of the questions asked are stories from
past  experiences  (life  as  well  as  work  ex).  All  these  stories  are  then  fit  in  the  leadership
framework  by  the  interviewer  on-spot.  The  interview  is  very  informal  and  chilled  out.  The
interviewers are usually team-mates that you will be working with.

Apart from experiences, scenario-based cases are given (same as consulting cases). Usually, no
general knowledge questions are asked. Recent advances in technology are also not asked.

6.  Any tips for preparations

Learn  Amazon’s  leadership  framework  and  weave  stories  around  them.  If  possible,  prepare  a
suggestion  to  pitch  to  them  regarding  Amazon’s  services.  Of  course,  do  not  pitch  your
suggestion directly but push it in between conversation.

8.  How  was  your  experience  at  Amazon?  Company  Culture?  Quality  of  work?  Senior

management exposure?
Mixed  experience  at  Amazon.  There  is  a  lot  of  learning  but  at  the  same  time,  work  is  usually

each individual is given full ownership of the project. There is no hand holding by manager or

peers.  Amazon  follows  an  open  office  culture.  Everyone  is  accessible,  both  formally  and

resources of the company are at your disposal and you are treated like a full-time employee.

My team  consisted of  3  individuals and one more joined while  I was  working  there. The  team
was  created  afresh  for  the  project.  All  teammates  were  industry  experienced  but  new  at
Amazon.

No project will contain two interns so there is usually no clash. Also, there is no fixed quota for
PPO roll-outs each year. If the whole batch is good, it will be absorbed.
